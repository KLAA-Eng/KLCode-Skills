# Discovery lenses and evidence

Select lenses; do not mechanically run every framework.

Divergence: invert an assumption, remove a constraint, shift audience, combine adjacent ideas, simplify radically, imagine the ambitious version, or ask what a domain expert sees. Generate several meaningfully different directions, then converge on the ones the user values.

Evaluate: acute/frequent pain versus convenience; named users and actual workaround; technical/resource feasibility; critical path; time-to-first-value; meaningful differentiation; third-party dependencies; smallest experiment; downside and cost of being wrong.

Product diagnosis: specific actor, pain/consequence, why now, ambitious version, MVP, anti-goal and success evidence. A go/no-go recommendation must expose assumptions.

Existing-product review: inspect usage, retention and revenue evidence if supplied/available. Missing data is unknown; framework imports and billing code do not prove demand. Identify the strongest improvement hypothesis without inventing numeric product-market-fit scores.

Journey review: inspect the actual onboarding path, record friction and time to first useful outcome, distinguish observed errors from inferred preferences and recommend a few concrete fixes. Do not install or remotely mutate systems without task authorization.

Prioritization: compare impact, confidence and effort against the same scale. A relative impact × confidence / effort score can aid ranking but does not replace dependencies, capacity or policy constraints. Explain sensitivity where uncertain scores change the winner.
